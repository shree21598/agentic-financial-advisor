# Bonds and Interest-Rate Risk

A bond is a loan to a government or company that pays interest (the coupon) and returns principal at maturity.

## Interest-rate risk and duration
Bond prices move inversely to interest rates. When rates rise, existing bonds with lower coupons become less attractive and their prices fall. Duration measures this sensitivity: a bond fund with a duration of 6 years would be expected to lose roughly 6% in price if rates rose by one percentage point. Short-term bonds have lower duration and less rate risk.

## Credit risk
Credit risk is the chance that the issuer fails to pay. U.S. Treasury bonds are considered to have minimal credit risk. Corporate bonds pay higher yields to compensate for more credit risk, and high-yield ("junk") bonds carry the most.

## Role in a portfolio
High-quality bonds provide income and tend to be less volatile than stocks, which is why they are used to dampen portfolio swings. Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) adjust principal with inflation and help protect purchasing power.
