# Taxes and Investing (U.S.)

## Capital gains
Profits from selling investments held more than one year are long-term capital gains and are taxed at lower rates than ordinary income for most taxpayers. Gains on assets held one year or less are short-term and taxed as ordinary income.

## Tax-loss harvesting
Tax-loss harvesting sells an investment at a loss to offset realized capital gains, and up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year, with unused losses carried forward. The wash-sale rule disallows the loss if a substantially identical security is purchased within 30 days before or after the sale.

## Asset location
Asset location places tax-inefficient investments, such as taxable bonds and REITs, in tax-advantaged accounts and tax-efficient investments, such as broad stock index funds, in taxable accounts. This can improve after-tax returns without changing the overall allocation.

## Health Savings Accounts
For people enrolled in a high-deductible health plan, an HSA offers a deduction on contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses.
