# Index Funds, ETFs and Fees

An index fund holds the securities in a market index, such as a total stock market or S&P 500 index, rather than trying to pick winners. Index funds are offered as mutual funds or exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

## Why fees matter
The expense ratio is the annual fee charged as a percentage of assets. A 1% fee versus a 0.05% fee may look small, but over decades the difference compounds into a large share of ending wealth. Research such as the S&P SPIVA scorecards has repeatedly found that most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index over long periods, largely due to higher costs.

## ETFs vs. mutual funds
ETFs trade on an exchange throughout the day like stocks, while mutual funds price once per day at the net asset value. ETFs are often more tax-efficient in taxable accounts because of how shares are created and redeemed. Both can be low-cost index vehicles.

## Total market and international funds
A total U.S. stock market fund provides broad domestic exposure in a single holding. Adding an international stock fund diversifies across countries and currencies. A total bond market fund provides broad investment-grade bond exposure.
