# Emergency Funds

An emergency fund is cash set aside for unexpected expenses such as job loss, medical bills, or urgent car and home repairs. A common rule of thumb is to hold three to six months of essential living expenses. People with variable income, a single household income, or dependents often target six to twelve months.

## Where to keep it
Emergency savings should be safe and liquid. High-yield savings accounts and money market accounts are typical choices because the balance does not fluctuate with the stock market and can be accessed within a day or two. Investing an emergency fund in stocks is generally discouraged because a market decline can coincide with the moment the money is needed.

## Building one
Start with a smaller goal, such as $1,000 or one month of expenses, then build toward the full target. Automating a transfer on payday makes progress consistent. Many planners suggest building at least a starter emergency fund before aggressively paying down low-interest debt or investing beyond an employer match.
