# Asset Allocation and Diversification

Asset allocation is how a portfolio is divided among asset classes such as stocks, bonds, and cash. It is the main driver of a portfolio's long-term risk and return. Stocks have historically offered higher long-term returns with higher volatility; bonds have offered lower returns with lower volatility and often cushion portfolios during equity downturns.

## Time horizon and risk tolerance
Investors with long time horizons can usually tolerate more stock exposure because they have time to recover from downturns. Investors close to needing their money typically hold more bonds and cash. Risk tolerance also depends on the investor's emotional ability to stay invested during declines; selling in a panic can lock in losses.

## Rules of thumb
A traditional rule of thumb sets the bond percentage roughly equal to one's age, so a 30-year-old would hold about 70% stocks. Many modern target-date funds hold more stock than this rule implies. These are starting points, not personalized recommendations.

## Diversification
Diversification spreads money across many securities, sectors, and geographies so that a single failure does not devastate the portfolio. Combining assets with low or negative correlation can reduce portfolio volatility without proportionally reducing expected return. Diversification does not eliminate market risk, which affects nearly all stocks together.
