# Measuring Investment Risk

## Volatility
Volatility is usually measured as the annualized standard deviation of returns. Higher volatility means returns swing more widely around their average. Daily volatility is annualized by multiplying by the square root of the number of trading days, about 252.

## Maximum drawdown
Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline over a period. It captures the worst loss an investor would have experienced and is often more intuitive than standard deviation.

## Sharpe ratio
The Sharpe ratio is the portfolio return minus the risk-free rate, divided by volatility. It measures return per unit of risk. Higher is better; comparisons are only meaningful over the same period.

## Correlation
Correlation ranges from -1 to +1 and describes how two assets move together. Combining assets with correlation below +1 reduces portfolio volatility, which is the mathematical basis of diversification.

## Beta
Beta measures sensitivity to the overall market. A beta of 1.2 suggests the asset tends to move 20% more than the market.
