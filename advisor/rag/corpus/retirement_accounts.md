# Retirement Accounts (U.S.)

## Employer plans: 401(k) and 403(b)
Employer-sponsored plans let employees contribute part of each paycheck. Traditional contributions are made pre-tax and taxed on withdrawal; Roth contributions are made after-tax and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. Many employers offer a matching contribution, for example 50% of contributions up to 6% of salary. Capturing the full employer match is widely considered one of the highest-return moves available, since it is effectively an immediate return on the money contributed.

## Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs)
Traditional IRAs may offer a tax deduction for contributions, depending on income and workplace plan coverage. Roth IRAs are funded with after-tax money and have income limits for direct contributions. Annual contribution limits for 401(k)s and IRAs are set by the IRS and adjusted periodically for inflation, so current limits should be checked on irs.gov.

## Traditional vs. Roth
Roth contributions tend to be attractive when someone expects a higher tax rate in retirement than today, such as early-career workers. Traditional contributions tend to be attractive when today's tax rate is higher than the expected retirement rate. Holding both provides tax diversification.

## Early withdrawals
Withdrawals before age 59 1/2 from retirement accounts are generally subject to income tax and a 10% penalty, with some exceptions. Retirement accounts are therefore a poor place to keep emergency money.
