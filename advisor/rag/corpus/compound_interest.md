# Compound Interest and Time Value of Money

Compound interest means earning returns on both the original principal and on previously earned returns. Over long periods compounding dominates: the growth in later years is much larger than in early years because the base keeps growing.

## Rule of 72
The Rule of 72 estimates how long it takes money to double: divide 72 by the annual return in percent. At 6% per year money doubles in roughly 12 years; at 9% in roughly 8 years. It is an approximation that works best for rates between about 4% and 12%.

## Starting early
Because of compounding, contributions made early in a career have more time to grow. Someone who invests for 40 years typically ends with far more than someone who invests the same monthly amount for 30 years, even though total contributions differ by only a quarter.

## Nominal vs. real returns
Nominal returns are not adjusted for inflation. Real returns subtract inflation and better reflect purchasing power. Long-range projections are often made in real terms, using a lower assumed return.
