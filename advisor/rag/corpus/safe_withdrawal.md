# Retirement Income and the 4% Rule

The "4% rule" comes from research by William Bengen (1994) and the later Trinity Study. It found that a retiree who withdrew 4% of a balanced portfolio in the first year, then adjusted that dollar amount for inflation each year, historically would not have run out of money over most 30-year periods in U.S. market history.

## Estimating a target nest egg
Dividing desired annual spending by the withdrawal rate gives a rough savings target. With a 4% rate this is the same as multiplying annual spending by 25. For example, $40,000 of annual spending implies a target of about $1,000,000.

## Caveats
The 4% rule is a historical guideline, not a guarantee. Longer retirements (such as 40+ years for early retirees), high fees, low future returns, or a poor sequence of returns early in retirement may justify a lower rate such as 3-3.5%. Flexible spending rules that cut withdrawals after bad years can improve sustainability. Social Security and pensions reduce the amount the portfolio must supply.
