# Rebalancing and Dollar-Cost Averaging

## Rebalancing
As markets move, a portfolio drifts away from its target allocation; after a stock rally, for example, a 60/40 portfolio may become 70/30 and riskier than intended. Rebalancing sells some of what has grown and buys what has lagged to restore the target. Common approaches are calendar rebalancing (for example once a year) and threshold rebalancing (when an asset class drifts more than about 5 percentage points). Directing new contributions toward underweight assets can rebalance without selling, which avoids realizing taxable gains.

## Dollar-cost averaging
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) invests a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price, which buys more shares when prices are low and fewer when they are high. Contributing from each paycheck is a natural form of DCA. When a lump sum is already available, research such as Vanguard's has found that investing it all at once has historically outperformed spreading it out about two-thirds of the time, because markets tend to rise over time; DCA mainly reduces regret and the risk of investing right before a decline.
