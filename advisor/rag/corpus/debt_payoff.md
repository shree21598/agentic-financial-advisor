# Paying Off Debt: Avalanche vs. Snowball

When someone has several debts, they usually pay the minimum on all of them and direct any extra money to one target debt at a time.

## Debt avalanche
The avalanche method sends extra payments to the debt with the highest interest rate first. Mathematically it minimizes total interest paid and usually shortens the payoff timeline.

## Debt snowball
The snowball method sends extra payments to the smallest balance first, regardless of rate. Eliminating small debts quickly creates motivating wins. It typically costs somewhat more interest than the avalanche, but behavioral research suggests the momentum helps some people stick with the plan.

## Prioritising debt vs. investing
High-interest debt such as credit cards, which often carry APRs above 20%, is generally worth paying off before investing beyond an employer match, because the guaranteed "return" of eliminating that interest exceeds expected market returns. Low-interest debt, such as a mortgage around 3-4%, is often paid on schedule while investing in parallel.

## Other tools
Balance-transfer cards and debt consolidation loans can lower the interest rate, but fees, promotional-period expirations, and the temptation to run up new balances are important risks.
